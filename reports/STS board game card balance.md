# Tiny Numbers Price Every Awakened Card

Slay the Spire: The Board Game balances everything on a very small scale. Characters have **9–10 HP**, start each turn with **3 Energy (max 6)** and **draw 5 cards**, a Strike deals **1 damage** and a Defend gives **1 Block**. Every buff and debuff is a capped token: Strength max 8, Weak and Vulnerable max 3, Block max 20, Poison max 30 across all enemies. Vulnerable doubles the next attack, and Strength adds +1 to every hit. On that scale the real cards follow a steady curve. A common or uncommon gives about **cost + 1 in its main number plus one small bonus effect worth about 1 point**, which works out to roughly 2 damage or 2 Block per Energy. Rares run at about **2 × cost + 1**, and starter cards sit one point below the common line. In the same currency, a card draw is worth about 1 point, a one-time Energy about 2, Weak about 1, Vulnerable about 2, and a permanent Strength about 2 Energy. Each drawback (Exhaust, Ethereal, a Dazed, a play condition) buys roughly one extra point. The designers protected this curve in three ways: every card that makes Energy either Exhausts or works once per turn, generated cards became capped tokens, and Powers that give a permanent stat were pushed to 2–3 Energy. As a result players call very few official cards broken, and nearly all of those are engines that stack per-hit bonuses or loop Energy and draw. Measured against this price list, most current Awakened cards are on the curve. Thirteen still are not. Nine are too strong, led by Song of Sorrow, Planeswalk and Thaumaturgy, which each break one of the curve's protections (uncapped triggers, repeatable Energy, a cheap permanent-stat Power). Four are too weak: Aphotic Fount, Arcane Nesting, Darkness Falls and Daggerstorm. Several rules texts also need a decision before playtesting: Rebirth can trigger more than once, Sludge Bomb conflicts with the official Dazed rule, and nothing says whether Manaburn stays on an enemy. One caveat applies to all of this: the prices come from the designers' own card pricing, not from win-rate data, and no win-rate data exists.

## Nine hit points make every single point a big swing

The rulebook numbers are what give a single point of card value its weight. Ironclad starts at **10 HP** and Silent, Defect and Watcher at **9** ([Rulebook](https://contentiongames.com/_images/STS_KS_Rulebook.pdf); [hieu-lee rules transcription](https://github.com/hieu-lee/slay-the-spire-the-boardgame-the-game/blob/master/docs/rules.md)). Each turn you set Energy to 3 and Block to 0 and draw 5 cards, with **no maximum hand size**. You can never hold more than 6 Energy. Unplayed cards are discarded at end of turn unless they Retain, and Powers stay out for the rest of the combat ([Rulebook](https://contentiongames.com/_images/STS_KS_Rulebook.pdf)). The video game's numbers were cut by about 5:1, so Strike goes from 6 to 1, Defend from 5 to 1 and Strike+ from 9 to 2 ([Meeple Mountain](https://www.meeplemountain.com/reviews/slay-the-spire-the-board-game/); [BGG](https://boardgamegeek.com/thread/3439957/my-thoughts-after-100-hours-with-slay-the-spire-th)). A comparison of 76 attacks with both versions gives a median video-game-to-board-game damage ratio of **4.7**, and 37 block cards give a median of **4.0** ([compendium](https://rustywolf.github.io/sts/)). Player HP was cut further, about 8:1, so every point of damage and Block is a larger share of a character's life than in the video game.

Status effects changed from timed debuffs into tokens that get used up, and that change reshapes their value. **Vulnerable doubles every hit of the next single attack, then one token is removed.** Strength is added before the doubling. **Weak gives −1 to each hit of the next attack, then one token is removed.** Weak and Vulnerable never wear off at end of round. **Strength adds +1 to every hit**, so each hit icon on a card multiplies it, while effects that say "deal X damage" or "lose HP" are not hits and ignore Strength and Vulnerable. **Poison never decays** and ticks at the end of the player turn. Block does not stop "lose HP" effects ([Rulebook](https://contentiongames.com/_images/STS_KS_Rulebook.pdf)). If a token runs out, the effect is simply ignored, and the token limits are shared between players. Card-copy effects cost nothing, are affected by Vulnerable and Weak separately, cannot be copied again, and only one "play multiple times" effect applies to a card. Cards also "have no memory" once they leave your hand; the only exception is cards that change their own cost, such as Streamline ([Rulebook](https://contentiongames.com/_images/STS_KS_Rulebook.pdf)).

Several status-card rules bear directly on the Awakened conversion. **Dazed goes on top of your draw pile. It is Unplayable and Ethereal, and when it is Exhausted it returns to the shared Daze deck, not to your exhaust pile.** The box holds only **10 Dazed**. The other status is the double-sided Burn/Slimed card; Slimed costs 1 to play and Exhausts ([Rulebook](https://contentiongames.com/_images/STS_KS_Rulebook.pdf); [BGG](https://boardgamegeek.com/thread/3566006/where-do-daze-cards-go-after-end-of-turn)). The game has no Void, Wound, Frail or Dexterity ([hieu-lee rules transcription](https://github.com/hieu-lee/slay-the-spire-the-boardgame-the-game/blob/master/docs/rules.md)). One shared die is rolled once per round, and it drives enemy intents and any card or relic that depends on the die. Players may target any enemy. The [aoe] icon hits every enemy in one row plus the Boss, while "ALL enemies" hits every row ([Rulebook](https://contentiongames.com/_images/STS_KS_Rulebook.pdf)).

| Rule | Number | What it does to card value |
|---|---|---|
| HP / Energy / draw | 9–10 HP, 3 E (max 6), draw 5, no hand limit | 1 point of damage or Block ≈ 10% of a life total; draw is never wasted |
| Strength | +1 per hit, max 8 | multi-hit cards and Strength-scaling hits multiply it |
| Vulnerable / Weak | ×2 / −1 per hit on the next attack, max 3 | Vulnerable is worth one whole attack; Weak cancels small hits outright |
| Block | resets each turn, max 20 | no Dexterity or Frail, so Block is never scaled or suppressed |
| Poison | never decays, 30 total | slow damage counted over the whole fight |
| Dazed | top of draw pile, Unplayable, Ethereal, 10 in box | costs you one draw; Exhausted Dazed leave your deck |

## Enemies scale by adding bodies, so each player's math stays constant

Normal enemies have fixed HP: **2–10 in Act I, 4–23 in Act II and 5–28 in Act III** (Transient is a special case at 99). Player count adds bodies, not HP: each player draws one encounter card, and its enemy is placed in that player's row. Only elites and bosses use the HP board, where the 1–4 player values rise almost exactly in proportion (for example, Gremlin Nob is 14/28/42/56) ([Rulebook](https://contentiongames.com/_images/STS_KS_Rulebook.pdf); [hieu-lee enemies.ts](https://github.com/hieu-lee/slay-the-spire-the-boardgame-the-game/blob/master/src/game/enemies.ts)). The two fan sources for these numbers (an unofficial digital port and a video-game mod) agree on every 1-player value but one Jaw Worm variant ([LuaViper mod](https://github.com/LuaViper/StSBoardGameMod/tree/main/src/main/java/BoardGame/monsters)). Bosses count as being in all rows. The digital port therefore has a boss's single-target attacks hit every player ([hieu-lee enemies-and-bosses](https://github.com/hieu-lee/slay-the-spire-the-boardgame-the-game/blob/master/docs/enemies-and-bosses.md)). That reading is well grounded, but the rulebook does not quote it directly.

| Act | Normal enemy HP | Typical hit | Elite HP per player | Boss HP per player (effective) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| I | 2–10 (a row with summons ≈ 7–15) | 1–4, about 2–2.5 per enemy per turn | 14–22 (Sentries: 3 × 7–8) | Hexaghost 36, Guardian 40, Slime Boss ≈ 42 |
| II | 4–23 | 2–5, about 3 per turn | 13–30 plus summons | Automaton 55, Collector 57, Champ 40 + 40 |
| III | 5–28 | 3–7, spikes to 10–15 | 30–35 (Giant Head 80) | Awakened One 50 + 50, Time Eater ≈ 90, Donu + Deca 100 |
| IV | — | — | Shield 30 / Spear 42 | Heart 100 (cannot drop below 50 while Invincible) |

Sources for the table: [hieu-lee enemies.ts](https://github.com/hieu-lee/slay-the-spire-the-boardgame-the-game/blob/master/src/game/enemies.ts) and [enemies-and-bosses](https://github.com/hieu-lee/slay-the-spire-the-boardgame-the-game/blob/master/docs/enemies-and-bosses.md). Ascension levels 1, 7, 10 and 12 raise elite, encounter and boss values on separate cards ([Rulebook](https://contentiongames.com/_images/STS_KS_Rulebook.pdf)).

Two consequences matter for card design. First, enemy HP was cut about 5–7:1 but **enemy attacks only about 3–5:1**. Measured against a 9-HP character, a hit is therefore about 1.5–2 times as threatening as in the video game, and each point of Block is worth more. This is my arithmetic from the tables, and players say the same thing: "cards that block for 3 or more are ridiculously good" ([BGG](https://boardgamegeek.com/thread/3386038/article/45012653)), and "monster HP increases far more than monster damage output" ([BGG](https://boardgamegeek.com/thread/3509604/article/46069889)). Second, almost every elite attack hits all players, and boss HP rises per player, so **per-player HP and per-player damage barely change with group size**. You can balance a card on solo numbers. The exception is anything that hits "ALL enemies" or every row: its total output grows with the number of bodies, so it gets stronger in multiplayer. There is no published fight-length figure. From a starter deck's output of about 3 per turn, an Act I hallway fight lasts roughly 3–5 rounds, an Act I boss 6–8, and Act III bosses need 8–12 damage per turn. That is my inference, not a sourced number.

## One energy buys two points and a bonus effect

The most useful single finding comes from pricing the 273 playable board game cards against each other ([compendium](https://rustywolf.github.io/sts/)). Of the 27 one-cost common/uncommon attacks with fixed damage, **18 deal exactly 2**. The ones that deal 1 carry a heavier bonus effect, and the only one that deals 3, Wild Strike, adds a Dazed. At 2 Energy the common/uncommon standard is 3 plus a real bonus effect (Clothesline 3 + Weak; Leg Sweep 3 Block + Weak). A clean 4 needs a drawback, as with Ethereal Carnage. Rares sit at about **2 × cost + 1**: Die Die Die is [aoe] 3 for 1, Immolate and Hyperbeam are [aoe] 5 for 2, Bludgeon is 7 for 3, and Impervious is 6 Block for 2. They usually also carry Exhaust or a condition. Starter cards are exactly one point under the common line, and their +1 upgrade brings them onto it. A least-squares fit over 54 clean cards (average error 0.26 Energy) prices damage at **0.53 E**, Block at **0.58 E**, a draw at **0.41 E**, Weak at **0.68 E**, Vulnerable at **1.31 E**, and one Energy gained at **0.74 E**. In the same fit, Exhaust is worth **−0.49 E**, a Dazed **−0.38 E**, and a hard play condition **−1.08 E**. Being a rare is worth about 0.6 E of free value ([compendium](https://rustywolf.github.io/sts/)).

Comparing pairs of real cards gives the same exchange rates. Shrug It Off (2 Block + draw) and Backflip (1 Block + draw 2) set **1 draw ≈ 1 Block**. Skim (draw 3 for 1 E) and Master of Strategy (draw 3 for 0, Exhaust) set **Exhaust on a pure draw card ≈ 1 Energy**. Seeing Red (1 E: gain 2 E, Exhaust) and TURBO (0 E: gain 2 E, a Dazed, Exhaust) show that a common Energy card **nets +1 Energy and Exhausts**, or nets +2 only with a drawback. Terror (1 E: Vulnerable, Exhaust) and Disarm (1 E: Weak Weak, Exhaust) put **Vulnerable at about two Weak**. Strength costs the most. Inflame is a **2-Energy Power** for one permanent [str] (1 Energy upgraded), and the only 1-Energy sources carry a drawback (Rupture loses 1 HP, Reprogram loses your Orbs, Spot Weakness depends on the die). Wish sets one [str] equal to 10 Block or 4 Miracles, so the designers value a permanent Strength at about 10 points over a combat ([compendium](https://rustywolf.github.io/sts/)).

Multi-hit, Powers and upgrades follow equally regular patterns. Splitting damage into more hits is free at base value: Twin Strike (1 1), Dagger Spray ([aoe] 1 1), Blade Dance (2 Shivs) and Riddle with Holes (4 Shivs for 2 E) all give **two hits per Energy**. The extra hits only pay off with per-hit bonuses. Even rare Ragnarok gets no premium on hit count, 5 hits for 3 E. Common/uncommon Powers cost **1 E for about one point per turn**: Metallicize (1 Block at end of turn), Combust (1 damage to a row once per turn), Noxious Fumes, Machine Learning. They cost **2 E for +1 Energy per turn or a permanent stat** (Fusion, Inflame, Footwork). Rare Powers reach Omega's 5 damage per turn or Demon Form's +1 [str] per turn at 3 E. Upgrades are **+1 to one number in 51% of cards** and **cost −1 in 16%**. Cost −1 is concentrated on Powers (19 of 54) and almost never used on Attacks (4 of 95). Removing Exhaust or Ethereal counts as a full upgrade, and no upgrade ever removes a Dazed or an HP cost ([compendium](https://rustywolf.github.io/sts/)).

## Designers capped engines, and players mostly agree it worked

No official costing guide or designer quote about the conversion was found; the designer podcasts exist only as audio ([Nerdlab](https://nerdlab-games.com/090-designing-slay-the-spire-the-boardgame-with-gary-dworetsky/)). The clearest evidence of how the designers thought is what they did. Co-designer "garyrocks" explained the infinite policy directly: "We removed most infinites… Tactician now Exhausts so that one is gone. Our main goal was to avoid annoying infinites" ([BGG](https://boardgamegeek.com/thread/3273438/article/44030408)). Players summarise the result as "hard limiting every single source of positive energy to once per turn except for Watcher leaving Calm" ([BGG](https://boardgamegeek.com/thread/3346448/article/44677929)). A reviewer adds that effects which once repeated "now happen only once per turn" ([The Kind GM](https://thekindgm.com/2025/07/18/boardgame-briefs-slay-the-spire/)). Other structural choices follow the same idea. Shivs and Miracles became capped tokens. Orbs no longer rotate. Wrath became +1 per hit instead of double damage, which leaves Vulnerable as the only doubling effect. Random video-game effects became die-threshold conditions such as Spot Weakness on 1–3 ([Rulebook](https://contentiongames.com/_images/STS_KS_Rulebook.pdf); [Space-Biff!](https://spacebiff.com/2024/06/11/slay-the-spire/)).

The designer's Tabletop Simulator playtest changelog (Nov 2022 – Oct 2023) is the closest thing to errata. It shows buffs aimed at "underused strategies, and getting more juice out of relics", followed two days later by "a couple of nerfs to dial back buffs that went too far". Specific changes include: the Loaded Die limited to triggering on a 6, Rupture+ reverted to cost 0 for one Strength, Mayhem made "once per turn", Sadistic Nature raised to cost 1, and Blasphemy nerfed ([Steam changelog](https://steamcommunity.com/sharedfiles/filedetails/changelog/2884027954); [page 3](https://steamcommunity.com/sharedfiles/filedetails/changelog/2884027954?p=3)). No standalone card errata document was found. The fixes were made through rulebook versions: v2.27 carried an Ascension 7 sticker fix, and v2.30 added teardown rules; the v2.30 details are unverified because BGG blocked access ([BGG](https://boardgamegeek.com/filepage/289836/slay-the-spire-official-rulebook-updated)).

Community consensus lines up with the price list. At the top of each character are card access and engines. For Ironclad that means Corruption, Dark Embrace, Offering and Heavy Blade plus Strength ([Think Gaming](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ZsUlEk_k4yc)). For Silent it is Acrobatics, Calculated Gamble, Adrenaline, Catalyst/Burst and Footwork ([Think Gaming](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=VzlJir7NGmU)). For Defect it is Defragment + Loop+ + one Frost orb, "basically hard walls you against most attacks" ([BGG](https://boardgamegeek.com/thread/3430701/article/45399886)). For Watcher it is Simmering Fury, Ragnarok and Wallop ([BGG](https://boardgamegeek.com/thread/3772772/article/48213374)). The bottom is starter Strikes and narrow conditional cards such as Blood for Blood, Masterful Stab, Grand Finale, Noxious Fumes ("F minus… auto skip") and Ghostly Armor. Ghostly Armor is the "closest thing this game has to a card that is strictly inferior to another" ([BGG](https://boardgamegeek.com/thread/3439204/article/45485776)). The short list of cards players call broken is all engines that multiply per-hit or per-token bonuses: Envenom with Shivs ([BGG](https://boardgamegeek.com/thread/3468618/article/45745802)), Simmering Fury with multi-hit Wrath attacks ([BGG](https://boardgamegeek.com/thread/3546242/article/46363346)), and Silent's Reflex/Prepared loops ([BGG](https://boardgamegeek.com/thread/3580195/article/46643907)). The Kickstarter Claw pack is "way broken" because it scales at three times the video-game rate ([BGG](https://boardgamegeek.com/thread/3673156/article/47529353)). Players also warn that slow Powers are penalised because fights are short, which is why Demon Form splits opinion: one tier list rates it F, while other players call it a boss-killer ([Think Gaming](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ZsUlEk_k4yc); [BGG](https://boardgamegeek.com/thread/3326011/article/44506020)). They also note that because most cards exist in only one copy, "new players… tend to undervalue the power of card access" ([BGG](https://boardgamegeek.com/thread/3507184/article/46047776)).

Fan designers have added benchmarks of their own. The Slayer Pack's author chose to "err on the slightly too strong side", and users judged its new cards "like 2–5% too strong" but offset by harder enemies ([BGG](https://boardgamegeek.com/thread/3368240/article/47592072); [BGG](https://boardgamegeek.com/thread/3368240/article/47612336)). A fan-character review counted **8–11 draw cards per official character pool** ([BGG](https://boardgamegeek.com/thread/3663793/article/48026957)). Another benchmark is that every character has a common row-AOE of about 2 damage (3 upgraded) for 1 Energy ([BGG](https://boardgamegeek.com/thread/3663793/article/47976999)). A fan Slay the Spire 2 Ironclad raised costs on Vulnerable cards "due to how Vulnerable works in the board game" ([BGG](https://boardgamegeek.com/thread/3702762/article/47633446)). The Awakened pool meets the draw benchmark (Peck, Clutch, Feather Flare, Chosen Verse, Mystic Order, Primacy, Skyward, Eclipse Embrace, plus Conjure). It meets the AOE benchmark through Pluck plus its Plume Jab. It has only one Vulnerable source, the Darkleech Spell.

## A price list for judging new cards

The table combines the official exchange rates with Awakened-specific rates I derived from them. The Awakened rows are my own estimates, not sourced figures. They rest on three assumptions: Chant means "if you played a Power this turn", Manaburn stays on an enemy the way Poison does, and a CAW deck plays one or two Power-counting cards per turn.

| Effect | Price (1 point = 1 damage = 1 Block) | Real anchor |
|---|---|---|
| 1-cost common/uncommon | 2 in the main number + one ~1-point bonus effect (≈ 3 points) | Pommel Strike, Shrug It Off, Headbutt |
| 2-cost common/uncommon | 3 + a real bonus effect (≈ 4–5); 4 needs a drawback | Clothesline, Leg Sweep, Carnage |
| 0-cost common/uncommon | 1 + a small bonus effect; 2 with Exhaust, self-top-deck or a cost penalty | Anger, Backstab, Masterful Stab |
| Rare | ≈ 2 × cost + 1, usually with a drawback | Die Die Die, Immolate, Bludgeon |
| Starter | 1 point under common | Strike, Defend, Bash |
| X-cost | X + 1 points (X if [aoe]) | Skewer, Whirlwind |
| Draw 1 | ≈ 1 point; draw 3 ≈ a 1-cost card | Skim, Backflip |
| One-time Energy | ≈ 2 points; a common Energy card nets +1 and Exhausts | Seeing Red, TURBO |
| Weak / Vulnerable | ≈ 1 / ≈ 2 points | Clothesline, Terror |
| Permanent [str] | ≈ 2 E as a Power; 1 E only with a drawback | Inflame, Rupture |
| Multi-hit | 1 hit per point; 2 hits per Energy at common | Twin Strike, Blade Dance |
| Power (common/uncommon) | 1 E ≈ 1 point per turn; 2 E ≈ +1 E per turn or a permanent stat | Metallicize, Combust, Fusion |
| Drawback | Exhaust / Dazed / Ethereal ≈ +1 point; 1 HP ≈ 1 E; hard condition ≈ +2 | Wild Strike, Rupture, Signature Move |
| Upgrade | +1 point, or cost −1 on Powers; +2 only on 2+ cost or rare | Strike+, Inflame+, Bash+ |
| **CAW!** (derived) | ⅓ of a permanent [str] ≈ 0.7 E ≈ 1.3 points, plus about 0.5 for counting as a Power | Desperate Prayer = Rupture's line |
| **Conjure** (derived) | ≈ 1.5 points: a known, Retained 1 E Spell worth about 3, still paid for | Thunderbolt = on the Exhaust line |
| **Drained** (derived) | ≈ −2 points (−1 E), about −1 in a deck that pays off Drains | Seeing Red in reverse |
| **Dazed** (derived) | −1 generic, about −0.5 or positive with Song of Sorrow / Eclipse Embrace / Gloomguard in play | Wild Strike, Power Through |
| **Manaburn** (derived) | ≈ 0.5 per point if it stays: it pays per Drain, and a Drain happens about every other turn | half of a non-decaying Poison |
| **Chant bonus** (derived) | about ⅔ of the bonus's face value | Watcher's Wrath/Calm-conditional bonuses |

The checklist that goes with the table has seven steps. First, sum a card's points and compare them with its line. Treat anything **two or more points** off the line as out of band, and anything one point off as a tuning knob for the upgrade. Second, any card that nets Energy or free card plays must **Exhaust or work once per turn**. This is the single most consistent official rule, and upgrades that remove Exhaust from Energy, draw or tutor cards are where it most often breaks. Third, any "whenever" trigger needs a **once-per-turn limit or a cube counter**, as on Combust, Distraction, Battle Hymn, Mayhem and Buffer ([compendium](https://rustywolf.github.io/sts/); [Steam changelog](https://steamcommunity.com/sharedfiles/filedetails/changelog/2884027954?p=3)). Fourth, multiply every hit icon by expected Strength. For the Awakened, that is roughly 1–3 [str] mid-fight from the CAW! track. Hits on Powers and on [aoe] cards multiply fastest. Fifth, price "ALL enemies" above [aoe], because it hits every row and grows with player count. Sixth, a +1 permanent stat is a 2-Energy effect. Seventh, every new token or card pile needs a cap and a removal rule, like Poison's 30 total and the 10 Dazed, because running out silently cancels the effect ([Rulebook](https://contentiongames.com/_images/STS_KS_Rulebook.pdf)).

Several cards sit about one point off their line: a real signal, but inside the "tuning knob" band. **Mantis+** and **Feather Flare+** give 2 damage for 0 E with no Exhaust. That is Anger+ without the self-top-deck, and Mantis+'s two hits double any Strength. **Soul Strike+** reaches 5[hit] for 1 E on turns with two Powers played, but costs 2 E on an average turn. **Extension** ties up no extra card slots, but it is still paid for with Energy on every replay, which puts it on Headbutt+'s line. **Eclipse Embrace+** works like Fusion plus Machine Learning for 1 E, but only on turns when a Dazed is Exhausted. **Sign in Blood** is strictly worse than colorless Master of Strategy (0 E: draw 3, Exhaust) because it adds a 1-HP cost; at this HP scale, 1 HP is priced at about 1 E. **Gather** is one point under Headbutt, **Cryostasis** one point under Thunderbolt, and **Brainshock** is Strike+ with a Dazed attached. The values of **Spreading Spores** and **Nihil** depend on the Manaburn rule. If Manaburn stays, Spores is a 0-Energy Noxious Fumes that stacks two per turn, and players already rate Noxious Fumes F-minus because fights are short ([Think Gaming](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=VzlJir7NGmU)). That suggests Spores is strong but not broken.

The round-3 review (`build/review/round3.json`) was saved after the last edit to `conversions.py`, so its flags are still open. This evaluation agrees with it on Thaumaturgy, 4th Dimension, Mana Shield, Aphotic Fount and Darkness Falls. It reaches a different verdict on six cards, each judged against its real counterpart. **Mystic Order** is on the curve, because Skim (draw 3 for 1 E) is the right comparison, not Burning Pact. **Byrd's Eye** is card-neutral: it replaces itself with a Spell you choose. **Envision** and **Midden Heap** sit on the 0-cost line; Midden Heap even thins a Dazed out of your draw pile. **Pluck** and **Initiation** match Cleave and Leg Sweep once CAW! and the Plume Jab are priced in.

Eight rules questions need a decision before testing, because they change card values more than any number does. **Rebirth** needs "once per combat" or "then remove this Power". **Sludge Bomb** "requires and removes Exhausted Dazed", but the official rule sends Exhausted Dazed back to the Daze deck, so no Exhausted Dazed ever stays in your exhaust pile ([Rulebook](https://contentiongames.com/_images/STS_KS_Rulebook.pdf)). **Manaburn** needs a rule for whether it stays or clears after it triggers, plus a cap like Poison's 30. Every Manaburn card's price moves by about half depending on that answer. The **10-card Dazed supply** is shared with enemy effects; when it runs out, effects are ignored ([hieu-lee rules transcription](https://github.com/hieu-lee/slay-the-spire-the-boardgame-the-game/blob/master/docs/rules.md)), which silently removes Planeswalk's drawback and Song's triggers. **Siphon** cannot steal Strength from the many enemies that have none. **Ensorcelate** ("the next Power costs 0") needs to say whether CAW! cards count as Powers for cost purposes. **The Encyclopedia** needs a physical source for its "random cards", since the board game has no random card generation. The **Chant** window ("this turn" or "this combat") should be printed on the player aid.

## Conclusion

The board game's balance comes less from any one number than from a few structural rules. Energy never loops, triggers are limited to once per turn, generated cards become capped tokens, and Strength is expensive because each point multiplies every hit. The Awakened's weak spots are the places where the conversion kept a video-game mechanic that those rules were designed to remove. Dazed became a resource with no cap on its payoffs. The one Energy card lost its Exhaust. Recursion and copying got round the ban on generating cards mid-combat. And Strength, the character's core resource, now feeds a Power that hits a whole row. Fixing Song of Sorrow, Planeswalk and the rules questions first would likely change the verdicts on Procession, 4th Dimension and Eclipse Embrace too, because those cards are strong mainly as multipliers on the Dazed-and-Energy engine.

One structural risk stays open whatever the numbers. Because each CAW! counts as a Power, 0-cost CAW! cards feed every Power-counting card at once: Chant, Awaken, Soul Strike, Skyward, Extension and Arcane Nesting. That makes the CAW! card the Awakened's equivalent of the Shiv, and it deserves the same token-style caution. After these changes, playtesting should look for single turns that are too big, not for average damage per energy. That is where official cards were broken (Silent infinites, Envenom on Shivs, the Claw pack), and where the Slayer Pack's "slightly too strong" approach was accepted only because its enemies were made harder at the same time.
